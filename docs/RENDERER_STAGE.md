## Consequences checked before edits

The original implementation reproduced stale Canvas tint pixels, stale same-size mutable GPU pixels, material-based painter-order changes and camera smoothing advancing on repeated reads. Later actual pixel checks exposed tinted nine-slice/mesh/shape differences, restored-context invalid-handle deletion, disabled shadow contribution and incorrect normal-map orientation. Allocation and streaming inspection found ineffective authoring budgets, retained surface/texture caches without sufficient bounds, and fallback acquisition on a canvas already assigned a WebGL context.

The repaired renderer keeps authored draw order, limits memory and allocation sizes, and may use a visible white fallback while a new texture waits for its configured upload budget. A changed already-uploaded texture retains its last valid pixels until its fair queue turn. A failed WebGL initialization may replace the DOM canvas to acquire Canvas2D; epoch guards reject stale async resets. Large backing surfaces are downscaled to explicit limits. The GPU residency ceiling is512 MiB even with streaming disabled; normalization, Ultra preset, the renderer and authoring maximum now share this same constant. Preload margin expands performance-culling bounds; without culling, all drawable-layer textures are already requested.

## Remaining explicit limits

- Actual26.15 native rendering comparison is pending the final versioned build. A13 executable must not be reused as15 evidence.
- Canvas triangle-clipped skinned meshes have a thin antialiased shared-edge seam. The fixture verifies interior color, shape, and alpha parity; it does not claim exact edge equality.
- Directional shadows are unsupported and must remain visibly identified by capability and ConfigPanel UI. Point/spot/area shadow and normal-map lighting use a limited CPU approximation.
- The mounted fallback case is programmer-injected GL createBuffer failure exercising actual production Vue lifecycle and pixels; it does not claim a spontaneous physical GPU-driver failure.
- These fixtures do not establish global absence of defects, exhaustive GPU-driver coverage, full export wiring, or user acceptance.
